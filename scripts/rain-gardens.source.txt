Measuring Runoff from Rain Gardens

Abstract. We compared how much water left planted and bare soil plots during a simulated storm. Planted plots released less water, with the largest differences in plots whose soil was dry before the test.

1. Methods. We measured stormwater leaving twelve plots after a controlled downpour. Six plots contained grasses and deep-rooted flowers. The other six contained bare soil from the same field. We collected the runoff in buckets for one hour.

2. Results. Planted plots released 18 liters less runoff per hour than bare plots. The difference was smallest when both kinds of plot had been watered the previous evening.

3. Interpretation. Deep roots and dry soil can help a plot absorb rainfall. The test used small plots under controlled conditions. Larger field trials would be needed to estimate the effect on a neighborhood drainage system.
